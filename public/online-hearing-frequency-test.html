Hearing loss at the very top of this range is extremely common with age and totally normal — it's one of the first frequency bands to go, long before it affects speech or music you'd notice day to day.

Wondering whether you or a loved one is missing more than you realise? This online hearing test plays beeps and spoken words and, in about 3 minutes, shows how well each ear is hearing. Treat it as a hearing screener and the first step in your hearing journey, not a verdict. Take the hearing test online with headphones for the cleanest result.

How the Free Online Hearing Test Works

Most versions of this online test follow the same pattern: answer a few questions, then follow the on-screen prompts from the comfort of your home. No email address is needed, and it takes a few minutes.

Pure Tones From Low-Pitched to High-Pitched

A frequency test plays beeps one ear at a time, from a low-pitched hum near 250 Hz to a high-pitched whistle near 8,000 Hz, getting quieter across several volume levels. The quietest sound you catch is your threshold for that pitch. Testing your ears individually matters, because loss often starts in one ear.

Speech in Background Noise

Other checks, like the digits test from one independent UK charity, play spoken numbers over white noise. Plenty of people pass a tone check yet struggle to follow a voice in a busy room.

Before You Start the Hearing Screener

Setup decides how much you can trust any hearing test online. Run the speaker test directly in your browser — it uses your camera or microphone live, and nothing you capture is ever uploaded.

Headphones and a Quiet Place

Wear headphones or earphones, not device speakers or built-in speakers, so each beep reaches one ear. Find a quiet place, or a quiet area with no TV running, and set your sound to its highest level. If you wear hearing aids, remove them first. That gives the most accurate results.

Who Should Skip This Hearing Test

These checks suit adults aged 18 and over with no existing diagnosis. If you live with tinnitus, ringing can mask the quietest beeps, so a clinic visit is the better route.

Reading Your Online Hearing Test Results

Most tools give broad bands such as good hearing or some loss. A pure-tone check gives an indication of your hearing ability at each pitch. Run the audio frequency sweep 20hz to 20khz directly in your browser — it uses your camera or microphone live, and nothing you capture is ever uploaded.

Pure-Tone Average and the WHO Hearing Loss Grades

Clinicians summarise each ear with the pure-tone average (PTA):

$$\text{PTA} = \frac{T_{500} + T_{1000} + T_{2000} + T_{4000}}{4}$$

Pure-Tone Average and the WHO Hearing Loss Grades
Ear500 Hz1,000 Hz2,000 Hz4,000 HzPTAGrade
Left1520304527.5 dBMild
Right1010152515 dBNormal

The World Health Organization grades a PTA under 20 dB as normal and 20 to under 35 dB as mild hearing loss. Over 35 dB in the better ear counts as disabling hearing loss, and severity climbs to 95 dB and beyond for people who are deaf. In this example the left ear is mild, and the 20 dB gap at 4,000 Hz is worth showing a clinician.

Why a Hearing Age Can Be Misleading

Some sites turn your score into a hearing age. That's misleading: loss affects every stage of life, and its cause may be loud music, infection or genetics rather than the years.

Running a Hearing Check After a Season of Band Rehearsals

I drum in a covers band, and after eight weeks of two-hour rehearsals my right ear, the one nearest the ride cymbal, feels dull in the car home. The decision I want to make is simple: is this worth a clinic visit, or just a reason to finally wear earplugs? I run the tone test on this page before deciding. The answer comes back as a right-ear pure-tone average of 32.5 dB, mild loss and an ear-to-ear gap big enough to book a proper test.

I use closed-back headphones, sit in the spare room with the door shut, and start the pure-tone check described above the evening after a rehearsal. The right ear's quietest beeps come in at 20, 25, 35 and 50 dB across 500, 1,000, 2,000 and 4,000 Hz; the left at 15, 15, 20 and 30 dB. I add them by hand to check the screen: 130 ÷ 4 = 32.5 dB on the right, 80 ÷ 4 = 20 dB on the left. Both sit inside the WHO mild band of 20 to under 35 dB, but the right ear is 20 dB worse at 4,000 Hz, the pitch where noise damage usually shows first.

Then I rerun it after 36 quiet hours:

Running a Hearing Check After a Season of Band Rehearsals
Right ear500 Hz1,000 Hz2,000 Hz4,000 HzPTA
Evening after rehearsal2025355032.5 dB
After 36 quiet hours2020304528.75 dB

That 3.75 dB recovery is a temporary threshold shift, and it's the trap here: testing straight after loud sound overstates the loss. The opposite trap matters more. Because most online tools step in 5 dB, the first run sat just two one-pitch steps from the 35 dB moderate line, so one misheard beep can move the grade. I'd trust the pattern, one ear worse at high pitches on both runs, far more than either average.

My judgement: the 4,000 Hz gap is the number to watch, not the average. Next step is an audiologist appointment with both screenshots, plus earplugs at rehearsal (NIOSH allows just 15 minutes at 100 dBA), and a retest on this page in three months to see whether that 4,000 Hz gap holds steady.

Signs of Hearing Loss a Hearing Check Can Confirm

  • Difficulty hearing conversations in restaurants
  • Asking people to repeat themselves
  • Turning up the volume on the television or radio
  • Struggling to follow a voice on the phone
  • Listening fatigue from trying to concentrate

Any of these hearing challenges is reason to run a free hearing check and keep the outcome for next year.

Why an Early Hearing Evaluation Protects Your Hearing Health

Untreated loss pulls people away from social settings, and that isolation is linked to anxiety, frustration and poorer mental health. Studies also tie mild loss to a higher risk of dementia. Spotting it early gives you time to treat it before your well-being suffers and you feel isolated. A screen like the one on this page is the quickest way to catch that early change: save your scores and repeat it once a year.

Online Test vs a Professional Hearing Test

A 3-minute hearing test can't replace an in-person test. It can't control your equipment or tell the type of hearing loss, so it's never a medical diagnosis. Only a full hearing evaluation can, and it takes longer than three minutes.

Book an Appointment After Your Online Hearing Test

If one ear scores worse or any loss shows, book an appointment with a hearing care professional at a hearing clinic. Bring a screenshot of your scores to plan next steps. Hearing care teams can also check for earwax.

Where Hearing Aids Fit In

If the clinic confirms a loss, modern devices amplify the pitches you're missing. An early screen shortens the years many people wait.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is this online hearing test free?
Yes. It costs nothing, needs no email address and takes about three minutes from the comfort of your home.
What do I need to take the test?
A quiet place, a pair of headphones or earphones, and your device volume turned up before you start. If you wear hearing aids, take them out first.
Can I take the hearing test with device speakers?
You can, but speakers play to both ears at once, so the result only reflects your better ear. Headphones test each ear individually and give more accurate results.
Can an online hearing test replace a professional hearing test?
No. It is a screening, not a medical diagnosis. Only an in-person evaluation by a hearing care professional can tell the cause, type and severity of hearing loss.
What does the pure-tone average mean?
It is the average of the quietest beeps you heard at 500, 1,000, 2,000 and 4,000 Hz. Under 20 dB is normal on the WHO scale, and 20 to under 35 dB is mild hearing loss.
What are the signs of hearing loss?
Common signs include trouble following conversations, asking people to repeat themselves, turning up the TV or radio, difficulty on the phone and feeling tired after concentrating to listen.
Why doesn't this test give me a hearing age?
A hearing age can be misleading, because hearing loss happens at every age and often has causes other than getting older. The test reports each ear's level and grade instead.
