Three Methods to Connect Your Phone as a Webcam on PC or Mac

There are three reliable paths to turn your smartphone into a webcam — a connected camera for your computer. Pick the one that matches your devices and OS. Method 1: Use iPhone as Webcam on Mac with Continuity Camera

Apple's built-in connected camera feature is the smoothest phone webcam setup available — no third-party application needed, no connection cables required. Your iPhone appears as an automatic camera source in every Mac application the moment it detects the phone nearby. This is the easiest way to use your phone as a webcam for Mac users.

  1. Step 1: On your iPhone, open Settings → General → AirPlay & Handoff and confirm the connected camera feature is toggled on. Also ensure the local network and short-range connections are both active.
  2. Step 2: On your Mac, open System Settings → General and verify your Mac is signed into the same Apple ID as the iPhone. Both devices must be on the same network and have short-range connectivity enabled — you do not need them physically connected.
  3. Step 3: Mount your iPhone landscape or portrait using a MagSafe mount, a MacBook mount, or any standard tripod clip. Position the back-facing lens facing you for the sharpest image.
  4. Step 4: Open your video application (FaceTime, Zoom, Teams, or any camera application). The Mac will auto-detect the iPhone and may switch to it automatically. If not, navigate to the camera input options within the application and select your iPhone from the dropdown.
  5. Step 5: You can activate video effects like portrait-mode background blur, Studio Light, or Center Stage directly from the Mac menu bar's Control Center while the feature is active.
Note: If your iPhone doesn't appear as an option, toggle the short-range connection off and on again on both devices. The Handoff feature and AirPlay must both be enabled for auto-detection to work reliably.

Method 2: Use an Android Phone as a Webcam on a Desktop PC with DroidCam

The most flexible method for Android users on a desktop system is DroidCam, which supports both cable and local-network connectivity and works on desktop OSes and Linux. The no-cost tier offers solid image output while DroidCamX unlocks HD and additional controls.

  1. Step 1: Install the DroidCam application from the Google Play Store on your Android phone. Then download and install the DroidCam desktop client on your computer from the developer's official site.
  2. Step 2: For cable connection: Enable developer mode on your phone by opening the system options → About Phone → tap Build Number seven times. Then go to Developer Options and turn on USB debugging. Connect your phone to the computer with a cable and tap "Trust" on the trust device prompt that appears.
  3. Step 3: For local network: Ensure both your phone and computer are on the same network. Open the DroidCam phone application — it will display the device IP address and the DroidCam Port number (default: 4747). Enter these into the desktop client's connection screen.
  4. Step 4: Click Connect in the desktop client. The handset's camera feed will appear in the DroidCam preview window. Grant any camera permission prompt that appears on the phone.
  5. Step 5: Open Microsoft Teams, Zoom, or your preferred conferencing application and select DroidCam Source as the active webcam in its camera options. You can now join sessions using your handset's camera as a high-quality replacement webcam.
Note: If the DroidCam feed doesn't appear in your application's camera list, check your computer firewall configuration. The firewall may be blocking the DroidCam Port. Add an exception for port 4747 in the system firewall to resolve this.

Method 3: Use Windows Insider Smart Connect to Pair Your Android Wirelessly

Microsoft's native Smart Connect feature — rolling out through the Windows Insider program — lets you pair an Android phone as a connected camera over a short-range link with no extra application on the phone beyond Phone Link.

  1. Step 1: Enroll your computer in the Windows Insider Dev or Beta channel via Settings → Windows Update → Windows Insider Program. Install the latest preview build.
  2. Step 2: On your computer, navigate to Settings → Bluetooth & Devices. Ensure the short-range connection is on, then click Add Device. This step is where you manage your bluetooth devices pairing list.
  3. Step 3: On your Android phone, open the Phone Link application (or download it from the Play Store). Follow the on-screen pairing instructions — accept the pairing code shown on both devices to complete device pairing.
  4. Step 4: Once paired, return to Bluetooth & Devices on your computer. Your Android phone should appear in the list of linked devices. Select it and choose Use as Connected Camera.
  5. Step 5: Open any video application on your computer. Select your phone from the available webcams dropdown. The handset's camera feed is now live as a wire-free webcam over the short-range and local-network bridge, acting as a true remote camera for your desktop.
Note: Smart Connect is a Windows Only feature currently limited to Windows Insider builds. It is not yet available on macOS or stable release desktops.

How to Select Your Phone as the Active Camera in Video Apps

Switching Your Camera Source in Zoom, Teams, and Google Meet

Once your phone is connected using any method above, you need to select it as the active camera source inside your video conferencing application. The phone won't automatically replace the laptop camera in every app — you need to point the application at it explicitly. This screen sharing of your camera feed is what enables video calling with your phone camera pc setup.

  • Zoom: Click the arrow next to the camera icon in the controls (or go to the video options before the call). Under Camera, open the dropdown and select the phone-related entry — it may appear as your phone's name, "DroidCam Source 3," "Camo," or "iPhone Camera." Click Join with video to confirm.
  • Microsoft Teams: Click your profile picture → Settings → Devices. Under Camera, select the phone from the available webcams list. A preview will confirm the feed is active.
  • Google Meet: Click the three-dot menu → Settings → Video. Select the phone camera from the camera dropdown. The change takes effect immediately for your next session or ongoing call.

Tip: Always select the back-facing lens when your phone is mounted — it and its sensor deliver noticeably sharper, more color-accurate video than the front-facing selfie camera. If only the front camera appears, switch within the application itself (there is usually a flip-camera button or an option to select the phone's back-facing lens directly).

If the phone camera does not appear at all in the application dropdown, try: restarting the webcam application on both devices, checking that drivers are installed (some desktop clients install a virtual camera driver on the system), or rebooting the computer after initial installation. On desktop systems, check Device Manager for the virtual camera entry under Imaging Devices.

5 Tips to Get the Best Image Quality from Your Phone Camera

Connecting your phone is only half the equation. These tips ensure your handset camera actually looks its best once it's live — whether you're in a conferencing session, creating pre-recorded content for a broadcast platform, or doing live streaming to an audience channel. Good setup habits are the foundation of productivity when using your phone camera pc workflow.

Optimize Lighting for Sharper, More Professional Results

Optimize Lighting first — it's the single biggest variable in picture quality and overall image output. Face a window for soft, natural fill light, or invest in a ring light for consistent illumination regardless of time of day. Avoid sitting with a bright window or lamp behind you, which causes the auto-exposure to expose for the background and leaves your face dark. Good illumination can make a 1080p phone camera look like a professional-grade video feed. For studio recording or content creation sessions, consider a key-and-fill two-light setup to eliminate shadows.

Keep Your Phone Charged During Long Sessions

Keep Your Phone Charged throughout any extended call or broadcast. Acting as a webcam runs the processor hard and drains the battery faster than normal use. Plug in a charger or connect a power bank during sessions to prevent a sudden drop mid-session. If you're using a cable for wired connectivity, many applications will simultaneously charge the phone — check your specific application's documentation to confirm this behavior.

Use the Back-Facing Lens for High-Definition Webcam Output

The back-facing lens on virtually every phone — from budget Android to flagship iPhone — is optically superior to the front selfie lens. The back sensor is physically larger, has a wider aperture for better low-light performance, and often supports features like macro mode, daytime switching, and magnification without quality loss. Set your camera application to use the back-facing lens as the primary video source and mount the phone on a tripod or MagSafe mount so it faces you comfortably. This single change can transform your video from acceptable to genuinely high-definition output.

Stabilize Your Phone with a Mount for Rock-Solid Framing

Stabilize your phone with a dedicated mount to avoid shaky footage and inconsistent framing. A universal clamp tripod works for any desk, while the iPhone Mount with MagSafe for MacBooks snaps magnetically to the top of your MacBook display and integrates natively with the built-in connected camera feature — no adjustment needed once placed. Phone stabilization is especially critical for broadcaster and content creator setups where camera movement is distracting. If a MacBook mount isn't available, a small tabletop tripod angled toward your face works equally well and keeps the lens at eye level for a more natural appearance.

Stay on the Same Network to Reduce Latency

For cordless connection methods, both your phone and computer must be on the same local network — ideally a 5 GHz band for lower latency and higher throughput. A congested 2.4 GHz connection can introduce noticeable lag between your movements and the video feed, which is jarring during video calling. If you experience choppy output, switch to a cable connection for a stable, tethered experience with no network dependency. Cable also offers low latency that approaches zero delay, making it the preferred connection type for professional video production and video recording scenarios.

Fixing Common Problems When Using Your Phone as a Webcam

Even a well-configured phone webcam setup can encounter issues. Here are the most frequent problems and their practical fixes — drawn from real-world troubleshooting across all major methods.

Background Blocking Your Video Feed

If your background blocked video issue appears — meaning callers see a virtual background instead of you, or a solid color fills the frame — the likely cause is a conflict between the camera application and your conferencing tool's virtual background feature. Disable virtual backgrounds or background blurring in your video application's options first. Then confirm the camera permission has been granted to the desktop camera client under the phone's privacy options. A camera blocked warning in the desktop client usually means the camera is already in use by another process — close the phone's native camera application before connecting. Recheck your framing: if the display orientation doesn't match, the image may appear cropped or off-center.

Phone Screen Not Rotating as Expected

Phone screen rotating behavior depends on your display options. If you flip the phone from horizontal to vertical or from vertical to horizontal and the image on the computer doesn't rotate accordingly, check that auto-rotate is enabled in your phone's display options. On Android, swipe down to the quick panel and confirm the auto-rotate toggle is on. On iPhone, swipe into Control Center and make sure the rotation lock is off. Some camera applications lock the orientation to whatever was set at the time of connection — reconnect after enabling auto-rotate. For the built-in connected camera feature, landscape mode is generally recommended for the best framing.

Camera Permission Notification on Your Phone

When you first connect your phone as a webcam, a permission request dialog will appear on the display asking whether to allow the camera application or the connected computer to access the device's camera. This is a normal security step — tap Allow or Accept to grant camera permission. If you dismissed it without accepting, the phone will not transmit video even though it appears connected. To see the permission prompt again, disconnect and reconnect in the application, or manually grant camera access under the application manager → [App Name] → Permissions on Android, or Privacy & Security → Camera on iPhone. The redirect notification you may see on some devices (where tapping the notification opens the application rather than showing the permission dialog) is a known behavior — simply open the camera application directly and accept from within it.

Note: If your phone is not detected at all after following all steps, try reinstalling the desktop client, rebooting both devices, and checking that the correct drivers installed during setup. For DroidCam on cable, re-enable USB debugging — some Android updates reset developer options and revoke the cable connection trust. For cordless setups, temporarily disable the computer firewall to test if connectivity is being blocked, then add a permanent exception once confirmed.

For Mac and Windows cross-platform needs, Camo, Iriun Webcam, or iVCam are excellent choices.

What is the best app for turning my phone into a webcam for online meetings?
For iPhone users, Camo delivers the best image output for conferencing sessions with manual controls and a clean picture. For Android, DroidCam or Iriun Webcam are reliable choices that work over both cable and local network. In all cases, after connecting, open your conferencing application and navigate to its video options to select the phone as your active camera source before you join the call. The join meeting experience is identical once the camera is selected — the application treats the phone's feed exactly like any other webcam.

Can I use the Camo app with Android phones and tablets?
Camo does offer Android support, but it is more limited than the iPhone experience. Some advanced features — such as full manual exposure control and certain video effects — may not be available on Android. For Android device and phone pairing, consider DroidCam or Iriun Webcam as more fully-featured alternatives. Samsung users running One UI 6.1 on a Galaxy phone can also use the native camera sharing feature to connect to a Galaxy device or Galaxy Book without any third-party application.

Can I use my phone as a webcam via USB?
Yes. A cable connection is actually the most stable and lowest-latency method. DroidCam, Camo, and iVCam all support cable connections. On Android, you'll need to enable USB debugging first. Simply plug into computer via cable, launch the desktop client, and connect phone to computer through the client's interface — it will detect the device automatically once debugging is enabled. Cable connections also tend to deliver the highest resolution and output clarity since there's no cordless compression.

Is DroidCam safe to use?
Yes — DroidCam is safe when downloaded from the Google Play Store or the official developer website (dev47apps.com). The application does not collect personal data beyond what's needed for the camera stream. However, avoid unofficial APK downloads from third-party sites, as repackaged versions may contain unwanted programs. Always grant only the camera permission the application requests — it does not require access to your contacts, messages, or storage to function as a phone camera solution.

What are the requirements for using Samsung's Camera Sharing feature?
Camera sharing on Galaxy phones requires the phone to run One UI 6.1 or higher. Both the Galaxy phone and the target device (Galaxy slate or Galaxy Book) must have local networking and short-range connections enabled, and the camera sharing toggle must be switched on in the connected devices options. This feature is only compatible with select Galaxy phones and is not available on non-Samsung Android devices. Once enabled, the phone acts as a portable webcam for any video application running on the paired device — including video calling via Google Meet — with no additional computer required beyond the Samsung ecosystem.

Why do I see a permission notification on my phone?
The notification appears because your operating system requires explicit camera permission before any external application or connected device can access the phone's camera hardware. This is a privacy safeguard. Tapping the notification opens the permission request dialog. If the notification disappears before you tap it, open your camera application directly on the phone and look for an in-app permission prompt. Dismissing the notification without accepting will prevent the webcam feed from starting — the device will show "disconnected" or no video output in the desktop client.

Can I use my phone as a secondary camera or second camera for video production?
Absolutely. This is one of the most powerful use cases — especially for content creator workflows, podcasting, and live streaming setups. Applications like Camo and DroidCam support multicam setups in OBS Studio and similar tools, letting you use your phone as a secondary input alongside a primary webcam or DSLR. You can also use it as a use as second camera angle for a different perspective, such as a wide-angle shot of your desk alongside a close-up face cam. For broadcaster setups, this eliminates the need for expensive additional camera hardware and turns your existing handset into a full phone camera input for your broadcast setup — truly letting you turn your smartphone into a webcam for any platform of choice.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can you use a smartphone as a webcam?
Yes — modern smartphones have cameras that far exceed most built-in laptop webcams. Using a free or low-cost app, you can connect your iPhone or Android phone to your PC or Mac via USB or Wi-Fi and use it as a high-quality webcam for video calls, streaming, or recording.
What are the requirements for using my phone as a webcam?
You need a compatible phone (iPhone or Android), the appropriate app installed on both your phone and computer, and either a USB cable or a shared Wi-Fi network. For iPhone on Mac (macOS Ventura+), Continuity Camera is built in and requires no extra app. Samsung Galaxy users on One UI 6.1+ can use the native Camera Sharing feature.
How do I use my iPhone as a webcam on a Mac?
If your Mac runs macOS Ventura or later and your iPhone runs iOS 16+, Apple's Continuity Camera feature works automatically over Wi-Fi — no app needed. Just unlock your iPhone near your Mac and it will appear as a camera option in FaceTime, Zoom, and other apps. For older systems, apps like Camo or EpocCam work well.
How do I use an Android phone as a webcam on Windows?
For Windows 11 with a recent Android phone, the Phone Link / Microsoft Phone app integration is the easiest route. Third-party apps like DroidCam and EpocCam also work on both Windows 10 and 11. Samsung Galaxy users (One UI 6.1+) can use the built-in Camera Sharing feature wirelessly with compatible Galaxy tablets or Galaxy Book laptops.
Can I use my phone as a webcam via USB?
Yes, connecting via USB is actually recommended for the lowest latency and most stable stream. Apps like DroidCam (Android) and EpocCam (iPhone) support USB connections. On Android, you'll need to enable USB Debugging in Developer Options. USB is ideal for live streaming or any use case where a dropped Wi-Fi signal would be disruptive.
What is the best app for using my phone as a webcam for Zoom?
For iPhone on Mac, Apple's built-in Continuity Camera is the best option — no setup needed. For Windows users or older Macs, Camo (iPhone) and DroidCam (Android) are highly rated. EpocCam is also popular and integrates directly as a virtual camera that Zoom, Teams, and Google Meet all detect automatically.
Why is my phone screen not rotating when I flip it?
Most webcam apps lock the orientation to prevent accidental rotation mid-call. Check if auto-rotate is enabled in your phone's quick settings panel. Some apps like Camo have an explicit orientation lock option in their settings that you may need to toggle off.
My video background is blocking my image — what should I do?
If a virtual background is obscuring you, check the background settings within your video call app (Zoom, Teams, etc.) and disable or change the background effect. The issue is with the call app's background processing, not the phone camera itself. Ensure adequate lighting so the app can properly detect your outline.
