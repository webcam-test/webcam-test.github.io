Brightness, Contrast and Saturation: The Webcam Filters Explained

The four webcam filters are deliberately simple filter options that correct a photo rather than decorate it. Turn saturation to 0% and you get a true black and white picture; nothing here adds grainy texture or colour casts. The turn your phone into a webcam covers the common cases in one place, so you don't have to piece the answer together from forum threads.

The Formulas Behind Each Slider

Each slider applies the standard CSS filter maths to every pixel channel \(C\) (0–255), in this order: brightness, contrast, saturation, blur. No computer vision is involved.

$$C_{b} = C \times b \qquad C_{c} = (C - 127.5) \times c + 127.5$$ $$R_{s} = (0.213 + 0.787s)R + (0.715 - 0.715s)G + (0.072 - 0.072s)B$$

Saturation keeps luminance fixed while moving each channel toward or away from grey, which is why these effects and filters never change how bright you look.

Blur for Skin Smoothing and Touch-Ups

A 1–2 px blur gives a soft focus that hides texture, a crude version of the skin smoothing in beauty filters. It cannot add makeup or targeted touch-ups, and above 4 px it looks like bokeh on your face.

Balancing a Window-Lit Tutoring Call With Webcam Effects

A maths tutor has a parent meeting at 4:15 on a laptop whose desk faces a north window. The question is simple: which brightness and contrast settings make both sides of the face readable without blowing out the lit side? The answer turns out to be brightness 112%, contrast 94% and saturation 104%, and the filter preview shows why before the call starts.

With the camera running, the tutor samples two spots from a downloaded still: the window-side forehead at RGB(203, 171, 152), luminance 176.4, and the shadow-side cheek at RGB(97, 72, 63), luminance 76.7. That 99.8-level gap is what makes the face look half lit.

The first instinct is brightness alone. At 112% the shadow cheek lifts to luminance 85.9, but the gap widens to 111.7, because brightness multiplies every value and bright values gain more. Dropping contrast to 94% pulls both sides toward mid-grey and brings the gap back to 105.0, with the shadow cheek at 88.4 and the forehead at 193.4. Saturation at 104% only restores a little warmth that the grey window light drains; it leaves luminance where it is.

The cross-check is the broadcast video range in ITU-R BT.709, where 235 is reference white. Solving the same formulas for the forehead red channel shows it crosses 235 at about 119% brightness, so 120% is already past the point where compression in a meeting app tends to flatten skin into a white patch. That is the number to watch here, not the shadow side.

The caveat: this webcam filter preview only shows the result locally. The tutor's next step is to enter brightness +12 and contrast −6 in the camera's own settings, then rerun the preview at neutral sliders to confirm the raw feed now matches. If the window brightens later in the day, the forehead crosses 235 sooner, so a lamp on the shadow side fixes the gap more reliably than any slider.

Live Effects vs Photo Booth Filters, AR Lenses and Masks

An online photo booth or a webcam toy layers fun effects and digital effects on top of the image. AR lenses and AR effects use augmented reality to track your face and pin masks, hairstyles, glasses, hats or beards to it. The photo booth approach is for play; tonal filters make you look better lit.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does the live filter preview cost?
Nothing. The webcam filters run free in your browser, with no account, no registration and no limit on how many stills you download.
Do I need to install anything to use these webcam filters?
No. There is no installation and no plugin; the preview runs in Chrome, Edge and other current browsers on a laptop, desktop, tablet or phone.
Why does my camera not show up?
Your browser has to be allowed to use the camera. Click Start Camera, choose allow access when asked, and close any other app (Zoom, Teams, OBS) that may already be holding the camera.
Does the preview change what people see in my video calls?
No. It only filters the copy shown on this page. To use the look in a call, copy the brightness, contrast and saturation values into your meeting app or camera settings, or use a virtual camera app.
Can I add stickers, frames or fun effects?
Not here. Photo booth tools add frames, stickers, AR masks and novelty effects such as fisheye or night vision; this tool sticks to tonal adjustments so you look natural, just better lit.
Is my video uploaded anywhere?
No. The camera stream is processed locally in your browser and the downloaded still is saved straight to your device.
Which settings fix a dark webcam image?
Start by raising brightness to around 110-120% and check that bright areas such as a window do not clip, then adjust contrast a few points either way and trim saturation if a lamp gives your skin an orange cast.
