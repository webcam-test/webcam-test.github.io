Before you touch a setting in your video-call app or streaming software, the Live Filter Preview lets you see what a brightness, contrast, saturation, or blur adjustment will actually look like on your own face — live, on your own webcam feed, right in your browser.

What the Live Filter Preview Actually Does

This tool applies four adjustments to your live camera feed in real time — brightness, contrast, saturation, and blur — and shows you a Before and After split so you can compare the unfiltered and filtered image side by side. Drag a slider and the After panel updates instantly, so you can judge the change by eye instead of guessing from a settings menu with no live preview. The front camera test is free to use with no sign-up, and works on both desktop and mobile browsers.

It's a preview tool, not a camera driver. Nothing here changes what your video-call software or streaming app actually receives — the adjustments only affect what you see on this page. Once you've found values you like, you note them down and apply the same brightness, contrast, or saturation change inside your call software's own camera settings, your OS camera settings, or your streaming software's filter panel.

Why Preview an Adjustment Before Applying It Elsewhere

Camera filter settings are easy to overdo. Too much saturation looks unnatural on video, and too much blur can soften real detail along with any background you're trying to hide. Most call software and camera apps let you change these values, but few show you a live before/after comparison while you adjust — you set a number, back out of the settings menu, and hope it looks right. Check the use phone as webcam if you want the background explanation before running a live test elsewhere on this site.

Previewing here first means you can try a few different combinations, watch the effect on your own live feed, and settle on values you're actually happy with before you're already on a call or already recording. It's also a fast way to compensate for lighting you can't control — a dim room, harsh overhead light, or a camera that renders skin tones a little flat — without buying new lighting equipment.

Saving What You See

Once the preview looks right, Download Filtered Photo saves a still image with those adjustments baked in — useful as a quick before/after reference, or just to check how a specific look reads in a static image rather than live video. The adjustment values themselves aren't saved anywhere else; if you want the same look in your call software or streaming app, you'll need to set the equivalent brightness, contrast, saturation, or blur values there. Run the webcam focus test directly in your browser — it uses your camera or microphone live, and nothing you capture is ever uploaded.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does this tool actually adjust?
Four things: brightness, contrast, saturation, and blur. Each has its own slider, and the After panel updates live as you drag it, so you can see the effect on your own camera feed as you adjust rather than guessing from a number.
Does this change what my camera actually sends to a video call?
No. This is a preview only — it doesn't touch your camera driver, and it doesn't change what your video-call or streaming software actually transmits. If you want the same look in a call, you need to set the equivalent brightness, contrast, or saturation values inside that software's own camera settings, or your OS camera settings.
Why would I preview a filter here instead of just adjusting settings in my call app?
Most call and streaming software lets you change these settings, but few show a live before/after comparison while you adjust. Previewing here first means you can try a few combinations and judge them by eye, on your own actual face, before you're already on a call or already recording.
What does the Download Filtered Photo button do?
It saves a still image with your current slider settings applied, as a quick reference for the look you landed on. It doesn't save or export the slider values themselves — note those down separately if you want to reproduce the same look in another app.
Why does my saturation or blur look worse than expected?
Both are easy to overdo. Too much saturation can look unnatural rather than vivid, and too much blur softens real detail along with any background you're trying to obscure. Small adjustments from the neutral midpoint usually look more natural than large ones.
Can this fix bad lighting?
Partially. Brightness and contrast adjustments can compensate somewhat for a dim room or flat-looking lighting, but they can't recover detail a camera never captured in the first place. For a harder lighting problem, an actual light source usually helps more than a software adjustment.
