Your face looks fine on your own screen, yet colleagues keep saying you look soft. The Sharpness and Focus Test measures that for you: it reads the edges in your webcam feed, turns them into a focus score from 0 to 100, and tells you whether the picture is sharp, slightly soft or blurry. How the Sharpness and Focus Test Scores Your Webcam

A sharp picture has hard edges; a blurred one smears them across several pixels. This webcam testing tool measures that in real time, on a square crop in the middle of the frame, and compares each frame with the sharpest frame the test has seen in this session: Nothing you do in the check webcam autofocus speed ever leaves your device — the whole test runs locally in your browser tab.

$$S = 100 \times \sqrt{\frac{\operatorname{Var}(\nabla^2 I)}{\operatorname{Var}_{\text{peak}}(\nabla^2 I)}}$$

Here \(\nabla^2 I\) is the Laplacian of the frame, \(\operatorname{Var}\) is its variance across the crop, and \(\operatorname{Var}_{\text{peak}}\) is the highest variance recorded so far. The square root turns variance back into a contour-strength scale, so a score of 50 means the edges are about half as strong as at the peak.

Laplacian Variance and the Live Preview

The Laplacian adds up the four neighbours of each pixel and subtracts four times the pixel itself. On a flat wall the result is near zero; on a crisp outline it swings far positive and negative. High variance means many strong outlines, which is what fine details such as eyelashes and printed text produce. While you watch the live preview, the browser repeats this on every frame. That is also how contrast-detect focusing works inside many webcams: the optics move until local contrast peaks. Grab a snapshot in the browser to compare a frozen frame with its score.

How to Test Your Webcam Step by Step

Nothing to install: you can test webcam online in your browser, and the webcam test takes under a minute: The webcam not working troubleshooting guide walks through the exact steps in plain language, with no camera or microphone access required to read it.

  1. Press start and allow camera access when the browser asks.
  2. Pick the right webcam if more than one is connected.
  3. Wait for the webcam viewer to show your live feed and the first score.
  4. Hold a printed page 50 to 70 cm from the camera and keep still for three seconds, so the session peak is set.
  5. Move back to your normal seat and read the score again.
  6. Use Take Photo or fullscreen view to inspect the frame yourself.

Set Lighting, Camera Angle and Eye Level First

The score is only fair if the scene stays the same. Keep the lighting steady, ideally a lamp or window in front of you rather than a bright background behind you. Set the camera angle so the webcam sits at eye level, about an arm's length away, and fix the framing before you start. If your lighting conditions change mid-test, restart it.

The Laplacian can't tell grain from detail, so the score jumped past 100. A reading above 100 means "fix the room first", not a sharper camera. Run any webcam test of this kind in steady, bright conditions: a 4K camera in a dark room still loses to a 720p one at a sunny desk.

Recording a Lecture Series: A Real Webcam Test Before Day One

I'm about to record twelve 40-minute statistics lectures with an external 1080p webcam clipped to a monitor 74 cm from my face, and the first draft clip looks soft. The decision is simple: keep this camera, or fix something before recording eight hours of footage. The answer came out as 74.2, slightly soft, fixable without buying anything.

First I set the session peak by holding the printed course outline 58 cm from the camera under my desk lamp. The tool logged a peak Laplacian variance of 731.8. Back in my seat it read 402.6, so the score is \(100 \times \sqrt{402.6 \div 731.8} = 74.2\). I recomputed that by hand and it matched. Since these are recordings, my bar is 85, which means I need a variance of at least \(0.85^2 \times 731.8 = 528.7\) at my seat.

Locking the focal position cleared the 528.7 threshold comfortably. The lamp-off rerun is the one to watch: 119.4 looks like an improvement, but it is grain, and the clip looks worse.

The less obvious problem is that the score is relative. It compares each frame with the best frame of this session, so it can't tell me the recording app had quietly picked 1280×720. I checked the pixels instead. With a 78-degree camera at 74 cm, the frame is about 1.20 m wide, so my roughly 63 mm eye spacing spans 100.9 pixels at 1920 wide but only 67.3 at 1280. The ISO/IEC 19794-5 face-image standard asks for at least 90 pixels between the eye centres, which the 720p stream misses even at a perfect score.

So my next step is precise: force 1920×1080 in the recorder, keep the focal position locked, leave the lamp on, and rerun before every session. Of all the inputs, resolution matters most here, because a score of 100 on a low-resolution stream only means "as sharp as this stream gets".

What Causes a Blurry Image or Poor Picture Quality

A low score almost always has one of three causes, and each leaves a different pattern in the numbers.

A Dirty Lens or Smudged Cover

Fingerprints and dust spread every outline, so the score stays low wherever you point the camera, as in row D. First, clean the camera lens with a dry microfibre cloth and check that a sliding shutter or screen protector isn't half over it.

Autofocus Hunting and Motion Blur

If the score swings while you sit still, the autofocus is hunting between you and the background, as in row C. Sit closer or turn off auto-framing, which keeps reframing the shot. If the score drops only when you move, that is motion blur from a slow shutter, often with lag and choppy movement. A Logitech or other external webcam usually lets you lock the focal position in its own app.

Low Light, Exposure and Grainy Video

In a dark room the camera lengthens exposure and boosts gain. The feed turns noisy, real outlines soften and the score is fooled upward, as row E showed. Watch for artifacting and flicker from mains-powered lamps too. Low-light grain is how a webcam can auto-adjust its way to blurry video that still looks bright.

Image Quality Readings: Resolution, Frame Rate and Color

Crispness is one part of image quality. A full test also reports these readings, and most of them move the score:

  • Resolution and video standard: the pixel grid, such as 1280×720 (HD). Fewer pixels across your face cap the score's real meaning.
  • Frame rate in fps: under 20 fps usually means longer exposures, so motion smears and the score drops.
  • Quality rating and number of colors: very few colours suggests heavy compression, which wipes out fine outlines.
  • Lightness and luminosity: a dark frame invites the gain noise that inflates the score.
  • Bitrate: caps video quality once a call app compresses the stream, so a high score here can still soften in a call.
  • Built-in microphone: no effect on the score, but moving the webcam back moves its mic too.

Supported Resolutions, Megapixels and Aspect Ratio

A webcam lists several supported resolutions, but a browser or app often picks a low one by default. 1280×720 is 0.92 megapixels, 1920×1080 is 2.07 and 4K is 8.29. Set the highest available resolution before you judge the score, and check that the aspect ratio is 16:9. A webcam resolution tester lists every mode the device supports.

Color Balance, Brightness and Field of View

Poor color balance or high saturation doesn't blur the picture, but it hides texture in skin and hair, lowering image clarity. Check brightness and color depth too. A wide field of view makes your face fill fewer pixels, so a 100-degree webcam looks softer than a 65-degree one at the same resolution.

Check Your Camera Permission, Software and Webcam Settings

If the test shows no picture at all, the problem is access, not the optics. The browser, the operating system and other software can each block the webcam, and the privacy settings on your computer can switch it off entirely.

Allowing Camera Access in Chrome, Safari, Firefox and Edge

In Chrome and Edge, click the icon left of the address bar and set the camera to Allow. In Safari, allow the camera in the website settings for this page. In Firefox, use the permissions icon in the address bar. On Windows, open Settings > Camera permissions; on Mac, open the Camera list in System Settings; on Linux, make sure your user can open the video device.

Drivers, USB Port and Hardware Faults

If the feed freezes or the score never appears, update the webcam drivers, then try another USB port or cable. Open the Windows Camera app or Photo Booth to see whether the system finds the device. If neither shows a picture on your laptop, try the camera on another computer, or use your smartphone as a webcam to rule out a hardware fault.

Testing Inside Zoom, Teams and Google Meet

Call apps add their own processing software, so finish by checking the app you actually use. Zoom and Google Meet each have a video preview in settings. Turn off background blur and "touch up" filters, which smooth outlines on purpose, and make a short recording. Test your webcam this way after any app update.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I run a sharpness and focus test online for free?
Yes. The test runs in your browser with no download or account. Allow camera access when asked and the score updates from your live video.
Why is my webcam blurry even though it works?
The usual causes are a smudged lens, autofocus locking on the background, low light that forces long exposures, or an app streaming at a low resolution. Clean the lens first, then check lighting and the resolution your app picked.
Why can my focus score go above 100?
The score compares each frame with the sharpest frame in the session. In a dim room the camera raises its gain, and the grain adds false edges, so the variance climbs past the peak. Add light and retest rather than reading it as extra sharpness.
Can a webcam pass a basic test but still look soft on calls?
Yes. A camera can show a picture and still struggle with focus, low light or motion. Call apps also compress the stream and may apply smoothing filters, so check the preview in the app you use.
Why does my frame rate drop in a dark room?
In low light the camera lengthens each exposure to gather more light, which leaves less time per frame. A 30 fps webcam can fall to 15 fps, and movement then smears.
Is my video uploaded during the test?
No. Every frame is analysed locally in your browser and nothing is stored or sent once you close the page.
Which browser works best for a webcam focus check?
Current versions of Chrome, Edge, Firefox and Safari all support camera access. If the camera is not detected, check the site permission in the browser and the camera privacy setting in your operating system.
