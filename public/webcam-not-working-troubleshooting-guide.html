Your call is about to start, you click join, and the preview window stays black. The indicator doesn't even light up. Webcam not working, right when it matters. This webcam troubleshooting guide takes you through the fixes in the order that solves most cases fastest: physical checks first, then the privacy improvements in modern operating systems that quietly block access, then drivers. Test after each step so you know exactly which change brought your picture back.

Quick Hardware Checks When Your Webcam Isn't Working

Before you open a single menu, rule out the physical causes. They take seconds to check, and they explain a surprising share of "my webcam isn't working" reports, including cases where the system insists the device is enabled and ready. The camera blur and sharpness checker needs a quick permission prompt the first time you run it, then gives you a live read-out in seconds.

  • Privacy shutter or kill switch: many laptops now ship with a sliding cover over the lens or a function key that cuts power to the camera. A closed shutter gives you a black or grey video feed while everything looks normal in software.
  • Cable and port: if you use an external webcam, unplug it, wait ten seconds and reconnect it to a different USB port, ideally one directly on the computer rather than a hub or monitor passthrough, which can underpower the device.
  • Smudges: a blurry, washed-out image is often just a fingerprint on the glass. Wipe it with a microfibre cloth before you blame the hardware.
  • A full restart: shut down and power back on instead of waking from sleep. It releases a device that a crashed process locked up and loads the driver from scratch.

If the indicator still stays dark after these checks, the camera usually isn't getting power or a signal at all, so skip ahead to the driver steps. If the light comes on but the picture is black, the cause is almost always permissions or another program.

Fix Camera Access in Your Windows and Mac Privacy Settings

Since Windows 10 version 1803, the operating system can revoke an app's permission to use your camera and microphone, and a feature upgrade can flip those switches off again without telling you. That makes the privacy settings the first software check worth making. On Windows 11: Read the compare webcam specifications for a clear breakdown you can refer back to whenever the same issue comes up again.

  1. Open Settings and go to Privacy & security, then Camera.
  2. Turn on the Camera access toggle switch for the whole device.
  3. Turn on Let apps access your camera.
  4. Go through the list of Microsoft Store apps and enable each one you use, then turn on the separate switch for desktop apps, which covers browsers and most conferencing software.

Windows 10 has the same switches under Privacy, then Camera, with a Change button above the device-wide toggle. On a Mac running macOS Ventura or later, open System Settings, choose Privacy & Security, then Camera, and enable the app you need; older versions keep the same list under System Preferences. Grant access only to programs you actually call from, and leave the rest switched off.

Close Any Other Camera App Holding the Feed

Some cameras and apps, especially on Windows, let only one program use the camera at a time. If Zoom, a browser tab or a background recorder grabbed it first, every other program sees a blank frame or reports the webcam not working. Quit those programs fully (check the system tray or menu bar, since many keep running after you close the window), then open the built-in camera app, Camera on Windows or Photo Booth on macOS, as a neutral test. A working picture there means the device is fine and the fault sits with the one program that fails.

Update the Camera Driver in Device Manager

If the camera stopped after a system upgrade or other change, an outdated or mismatched camera driver is the likely cause. To refresh it:

  1. Right-click Start and open Device Manager.
  2. Expand Cameras, Imaging devices or Sound, video and game controllers; where it appears depends on the model.
  3. Right-click your camera, choose Update driver, then pick the automatic search option.

If you're told the best driver is already installed, look in the optional section of Windows Update, then on the manufacturer support website for your laptop or camera model, which often carries a newer package than the generic one Microsoft supplies. Install it, reboot and test again. A device that doesn't appear in the list at all, even after you choose Show hidden devices from the View menu, points to a camera disabled in firmware or a physical fault rather than software.

Reinstall the Driver to Clear Driver Corruption

When updating changes nothing, driver corruption or a broken configuration may be to blame, and a clean reinstall usually clears it.

  1. In the same window, right-click the camera and choose Uninstall device. Tick the box to remove the driver software if it's offered (the wording differs slightly between versions 10 and 11), so this uninstall driver step clears the damaged files too.
  2. Confirm, then open the Action menu and select Scan for hardware changes, or simply reboot.
  3. Let the system detect the camera and install its default driver again, then test it before reopening your call software.

Webcam Troubleshooting Guide for When the Camera Stops Working Entirely

If the camera stops working in every app even after the steps above, run the built-in troubleshooter: on Windows 11 open Settings, then System, Troubleshoot, Other troubleshooters, and choose Camera (newer builds hand this off to the Get Help app). It checks permissions and drivers in one pass. Next, check whether the computer's firmware has the camera turned off. Some business laptops include a BIOS or UEFI option for it, and resetting firmware to defaults can disable it.

Still nothing? Try the camera on a second computer. If an external model fails there too, or a built-in one keeps disconnecting, flickering or freezing, the unit itself has failed and no setting will bring it back. For a built-in camera, check the warranty with the laptop maker; for an external one, replacing it is usually cheaper than a repair.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why is my webcam not being detected by my computer?
The most common reasons a webcam isn't detected include a loose or faulty USB connection, an outdated or corrupt driver, or a hardware conflict in Device Manager. For integrated webcams, a disabled device in BIOS or Device Manager is often the culprit. Try plugging into a different USB port, updating drivers, or running the Windows Camera Troubleshooter.
Should I troubleshoot an integrated webcam differently from an external one?
Yes. Integrated webcams are built into the laptop's motherboard and are more often affected by driver issues, BIOS settings, or privacy switches on the laptop itself. External USB webcams are additionally susceptible to cable faults, USB hub power issues, and port compatibility. Wireless webcams can also suffer from pairing and signal interference problems.
How do I fix a black screen on my webcam even though it's detected?
A black screen with the camera detected usually means another application has exclusive control of the camera, the lens cover is closed, or there's a driver rendering issue. Close all other apps that might use the camera, check for a physical privacy shutter, and try reinstalling the camera driver. On Windows, also check that the Camera app has permission in Privacy Settings.
Why does my webcam work in one app but not another?
This is almost always a permissions issue. Each application needs explicit permission to access the camera, especially on Windows 10/11 and macOS. Go to System Settings → Privacy → Camera and ensure the specific app is toggled on. Some apps also require you to select the correct camera from within their own settings.
How do I update webcam drivers on Windows?
Open Device Manager (right-click Start → Device Manager), expand 'Cameras' or 'Imaging Devices', right-click your webcam, and select 'Update driver'. Choose 'Search automatically for drivers'. You can also visit the manufacturer's website (e.g., Logitech, Microsoft) and download the latest driver directly for your model.
Can a Windows or macOS update break my webcam?
Yes, OS updates occasionally introduce driver incompatibilities that can break camera functionality. If your webcam stopped working right after a system update, try rolling back the camera driver in Device Manager (right-click → Properties → Driver tab → Roll Back Driver). You can also check the manufacturer's site for an updated driver compatible with your new OS version.
What error codes are common for webcam issues on Windows?
Common Windows webcam error codes include 0xA00F4244 (no cameras attached), 0xA00F4246 (camera in use by another app), and 0xA00F4292 (something went wrong). Each code points to a specific cause — the Windows Camera Troubleshooter can auto-detect and fix many of these. Searching the error code on Microsoft Support gives targeted fix steps.
How can I improve poor webcam video quality?
Poor quality is usually caused by bad lighting, low resolution settings, or a dirty lens. Ensure you have good front-facing light, clean the lens with a soft cloth, and check the app's video settings to confirm the highest resolution is selected. For USB webcams, using a USB 3.0 port and avoiding USB hubs can also improve bandwidth and frame rate.
