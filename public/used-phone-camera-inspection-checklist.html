A seller can make a screen look new, but the camera is harder to fake. This used phone camera inspection streams a live image from every camera in your browser, so as the buyer you can spot defects like bad pixels, haze and blurry shots before you pay. How the Used Phone Camera Inspection Works

Open the page on the phone you're buying, tap start and allow camera access when the browser asks. Keep the phone's own camera app closed so the camera test gets the hardware to itself. A full pass takes about six minutes: Nothing you do in the camera zoom test ever leaves your device — the whole test runs locally in your browser tab.

  • 0:00 – open the page and allow access
  • 0:40 – main rear lens on a bright, detailed scene
  • 1:50 – sweep through every rear unit
  • 3:10 – front camera, close and at arm's length
  • 4:00 – one shot in a dim room
  • 5:00 – a 10-second video clip, then review

Check the Rear Camera, Then the Front

Start with the main rear camera, since it does most of the work. Switch through the rear lenses one by one: the main unit, the ultra-wide and, on higher-end models, the telephoto. Then flip to the selfie camera.

Capture Test Images and Zoom In

The preview hides small flaws, so take a few photos and open them magnified to 100%, checking corners and plain areas. Scanning a barcode from 10 cm is a quick close-range check, and saved shots land in the camera roll like screenshots for a later look on a bigger screen.

Run It Privately on Android or iOS

The feed stays on the phone, on Android and iOS alike. Close any app using the camera in the background first, or the page may not get access.

Common Camera Module Defects to Look For

Some faults mean the camera module is failing; others are cheap fixes or simply dirt. Knowing which is which keeps you from walking away from a good phone or overpaying for damaged camera hardware. Malfunction: A Black Screen or Corrupted Image Buffer

The clearest malfunction is a camera that won't start or shows only black. A rarer sign is a picture full of stripes or fragments of another image, meaning the image buffer is being fed garbage. Either way it's a failed part, not a setting.

Hot or Dead Pixels on the Image Sensor

Aim at a plain, evenly lit surface and look for dots that stay put when you move the phone. Bright dots are hot; dark ones are dead. A couple are tolerable, but a cluster near the centre of the sensor shows up in every shot.

Spots or Haze from Dust and Cover Glass Scratches

Soft round blotches fixed in place usually mean dust inside the module. A milky haze over the whole picture points to the cover glass instead: fine scratches or grime on the outer window. Wipe the lens and retest. These artifacts often appear or vanish as you change zoom, because the phone hands off to a different physical camera.

Out-of-Focus Shots and a Stuck Autofocus

Tap to focus on something near, then across the room. If close objects are sharp but distant ones stay soft, or the reverse, the focusing mechanism is likely jammed. A faulty autofocus can still look fine at one distance, so an out-of-focus result at only one range still counts.

Slow Frame Updates and Missing Cameras

A choppy preview often means another app is holding the camera or the phone is overheating. If a rear unit never appears, recheck in the built-in app before calling it a fault; some pages only see some cameras.

A Camera Test That Caught a Faulty Telephoto on a Marketplace Phone

The decision on the table: pay $338 for a two-year-old phone with a 3x telephoto, bought mainly for concert shots from the back of a hall. The answer, ten minutes later, is an offer of $273.10, because the telephoto only focuses at close range.

I meet the seller at a café by the window, open the camera test on their phone and allow access. The main rear camera streams 1920×1080 at a steady 29.7 fps against a nominal 30, and the ultra-wide and front camera are clean. Then I tap 3x and frame a menu board about 1.2 m away. Sharp. On paper, a pass.

Here's the trap I watch for. A phone hands short-range zoom shots to the main camera with a digital crop, because the telephoto can't focus that close. So a 3x test at arm's length may never touch the telephoto at all. I step outside and aim at a street sign roughly 9 m away instead. Now the image stays soft whatever I tap, and covering the telephoto window with a finger blacks out the preview, which confirms that module is the one streaming.

And one caveat: in dim light many phones switch away from the telephoto too, so run that distance check in daylight or the result proves nothing.

Manual vs Automated Smartphone Camera Checks

A dead camera is obvious. Haze and soft detail are not, and two people can disagree about the same photo. That's the gap automated testing closes for resellers.

Why Manual Testing Needs Repeatable Conditions

Manual testing works best with a fixed scene: the same ambient lighting, distance and target for every phone, such as a uniform white sheet for spots and small print for detail. Compare the captured images side by side. A repeatable setup turns "looks a bit soft" into a fair call.

How Functional Testing Scores Sharpness

Refurbishing labs use functional testing rigs that photograph fixed targets under controlled light. A high-contrast chart gives a sharpness reading and a plain lit target a uniformity reading, so numerical analysis rates every unit the same way. Borrow the idea: shoot the same print and sheet on this phone and on one you trust.

Where Diagnostics Fit When You Buy a Pre-Owned Smartphone

Professional diagnostics check far more than the camera, but the camera is often where pre-owned smartphones hide their worst damage.

Private Sales and Marketplace Listings

On a marketplace listing, you're the one checking. Meet in daylight, run the test on the spot, and don't rely on the listing's photos. If photography is why you're buying a second-hand flagship, give the camera the most time.

Refurbishers, Buyback and Trade-In Programs

Refurbishers, carrier trade-in desks and buyback programs process used devices at scale. Their certified units have passed a standard check, and refurbished stock usually carries a warranty, but a two-minute camera check on arrival is still worth it.

Reading a Device History Report

Many resellers include a device history report. Start with the repair history: a replaced camera part tells you which unit to test hardest, and a non-original one on a device sold as untouched is a reason to ask questions. Then glance at the IMEI, carrier blacklist and battery lines.

Turning Camera Condition into a Fair Price

Say a three-year-old flagship is listed at an asking price of $412. The check finds faint haze on the ultra-wide only, from debris inside that unit, plus one hairline scratch on the camera cover. A repair shop quotes $88 for the module and $27 for the glass, so a fair offer is $412 − $88 − $27 = $297.

Grading Second-Hand Smartphone Cameras

Resellers fold the camera into an overall grade. A flawless camera keeps the top grade despite light body wear; a camera fault drops the phone a grade or more, whatever the cosmetic grade says. Rate the cameras on their own and use that grade, not the seller's description, to set your number.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I check the camera on a used phone before buying it?
Open the camera test in the phone's own browser, allow camera access and watch the live feed. Switch between the front and rear cameras, take a snapshot and record a short clip, then enter what you saw here to get a pass or fail for each check.
Is a browser camera test enough to inspect a used phone?
It's a strong first check: it confirms each camera is detected, streams at its expected resolution and captures a clean picture. For a deeper look, also shoot in dim light, zoom in on snapshots to look for dust, haze or scratches, and test focus at near and far distances.
Why does the rear or front camera not show up?
Usually the browser was denied camera permission, another app is holding the camera, or the phone only exposes some of its cameras to web pages. Check the Cameras Found count, close other camera apps and reload. If a camera is still missing, recheck it in the phone's built-in camera app.
What does the Resolution ≥ 720p check mean?
It passes when the live stream is at least 1280×720. A lower figure can mean a weak sensor, a damaged camera or the browser falling back to a reduced stream, so retest in good light before treating it as a fault.
Why is the frame rate low?
Frame rate is frames counted divided by the seconds counted. A low rate often means another app is using the camera in the background, the phone is throttling, or the room is too dark, since each frame then needs a longer exposure.
Why does the torch show N/A?
Torch control through the browser only works on some Android phones; iOS Safari doesn't expose it to web pages. N/A here doesn't mean the flash is broken, so test it in the phone's camera app instead.
Are my photos or videos uploaded anywhere?
No. The camera test runs locally in your browser. Snapshots and recordings stay on the phone, and closing the tab clears them.
