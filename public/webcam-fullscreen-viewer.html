How to Open Your Webcam in Full Screen Mode

The whole webcam test takes under a minute: Run the camera in use detector directly in your browser — it uses your camera or microphone live, and nothing you capture is ever uploaded.

  1. Pick your webcam from the dropdown menu if more than one is connected.
  2. Press Start and choose Allow on the pop-up.
  3. Check the preview window, then double click it (or use the expand button) to put the camera in full screen.
  4. Press Esc to return to the normal page.

Nothing leaves your machine: the stream is drawn onto the page and vanishes when you close the tab. It's the quickest way to test your camera on a laptop without opening a separate camera app.

Most video calls squeeze your own picture into a corner tile, which hides the faults you'd want to fix. A webcam viewer that fills the screen gives every sensor pixel room to be seen. Take a webcam sending 1280×720 to a 2560×1440 monitor. In a 400-pixel-wide tile, each webcam pixel is drawn at 0.31× its size, so only about one in ten reaches your eyes. Fullscreen, the same feed is drawn at 2× and covers roughly 41 times the area, which is where soft focus, compression blocks and grain become obvious.

Image Quality and Lighting in the Webcam Feed

Look for three things at full size: sharp edges on your eyes and hair, even skin tone with no blown-out forehead, and a dark background without crawling grain. Poor lighting is the usual cause of grain, because the sensor raises its gain to compensate; a lamp in front of you does more for video quality than a pricier webcam.

Position Your Webcam at Eye Level

Fullscreen also exposes framing. Set the lens level with your eyes, tilt it a few degrees down, and leave a little headroom so your head and upper body fill the frame without your hair touching the top edge. A low angle from a laptop lid looks up your nose; on a big monitor it's impossible to miss.

Checking an Interview Setup in the Webcam Viewer

It's the evening before a panel interview on Teams, and the question is simple: is the laptop's built-in camera good enough, or should the spare 1080p webcam in the drawer go on top of the monitor? The answer, after ten minutes: switch to the external webcam, because the built-in one is being stretched 2× and looks it.

The laptop is docked to a 34-inch ultrawide running 3440×1440. I open the fullscreen camera viewer, allow access, and expand the built-in camera's feed. It reports 1280×720, so the scale is the smaller of 3440 ÷ 1280 = 2.69 and 1440 ÷ 720 = 2.0. Height wins: the picture is drawn at 2.0× into a 2560×1440 block, with 440-pixel black bars on each side. At that size my eyelashes smear and the bookshelf behind me crawls with grain.

Webcam Troubleshooting When the Preview Stays Black

A black or frozen picture usually has one of four causes:

  • Another app is holding the webcam. Close your meeting tools and any other software that uses it, then reload the page.
  • An external webcam has a physical ON/OFF switch or lens cover that's closed.
  • The webcam isn't detected at all (next section).
  • Access was denied (the section after that).

Check Device Connections and Drivers

Make sure the cable is fully plugged in to a working USB port and the connection cable isn't kinked or frayed. Keep the operating system and webcam drivers up to date, and if the device still doesn't show, try another port or another computer to rule out the webcam itself.

Fix Browser Permissions on Mac and Windows

Click the lock or camera icon next to the address in Chrome, Safari or Firefox and set the webcam to Allow. If the browser permissions look right but the feed stays black, the operating system may be blocking it:

  • Mac: open System Preferences, go to Security and Privacy, choose Camera, and tick Chrome or Safari.
  • Windows 11: open Start › Settings › Privacy & security › Camera, then turn on Camera access and let desktop apps use it.
  • Windows 10: the same switches sit under Settings › Privacy › Camera; you can also run the camera troubleshooter in the Get Help app.

Show a Webcam Feed Fullscreen on Windows with VLC

For a picture that stays on a second screen for hours, such as a lectern monitor, a browser tab isn't ideal. The free VLC player can show a built-in web camera or an external webcam camera fullscreen without any extra software.

Open a Capture Device and Fix the Aspect Ratio

In VLC choose Media › Open Capture Device, pick your webcam under Video device name, and press Play. Open Advanced options if the picture looks squashed: the default aspect setting is often 4:3, while most webcams send 16:9. Force the correct format, then double-click the picture to fill the display. The reverse wastes space: a 640×480 (4:3) stream on a 1920×1080 display is scaled 2.25× to 1440×1080, leaving 240-pixel black bars on each side, a quarter of the width unused.

Network Cameras over RTSP, UDP or HTTP

A web page only reads cameras on your own machine, so for an IP cam this player gives you the same fullscreen check. Its Network tab (Media › Open Network Stream) plays a remote camera, such as an IP security cam or a phone streaming over RTSP or UDP, fullscreen, using the address the camera's own app gives you.

This free webcam test keeps everything private: nothing is uploaded or saved and there's no account, as any trustworthy online webcam test should work. The same page works on a Mac, Windows PC, Chromebook, iPhone or Android phone, so you can test your webcam on whichever device you'll actually call from. A quick cam test on the right hardware beats a perfect one on the wrong machine.

Microphone Check After a Webcam Test

Once the fullscreen view shows a sharp, well-lit picture, check the microphone too, because a sharp image with muffled audio still makes a poor call. Speak into your meeting tool's mic check and listen back; if nothing registers, the microphone permission sits beside the camera one in the same system settings.

  • Run the webcam test first, then the microphone check, so one fix doesn't hide the other.
  • Make a 10-second test recording to hear your audio the way others will.
  • To record a short practice take, use a screen recorder that can record your webcam, then compare it with what the fullscreen view showed.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I set my camera to full screen?
Start the viewer, allow camera access when your browser asks, then double click the live picture or press the expand button. Press Esc to leave fullscreen. On Windows you can also open the webcam in VLC through Media, Open Capture Device, and double click the video.
How do I test my webcam?
Press Start, choose Allow on the permission pop-up and pick your camera from the dropdown if you have more than one. If you see yourself, the webcam works; then check the resolution, frame rate and the picture at full size.
Why does my webcam look blurry in fullscreen?
The viewer is enlarging the stream to fit your screen. A 1280×720 feed on a 2560×1440 monitor is drawn at 2×, so every pixel is doubled. Better lighting, a higher-resolution webcam or a smaller window all help.
Why does my webcam seem so zoomed in?
Many apps crop the picture to fill a 16:9 tile, and a 4:3 camera loses its top and bottom when that happens. Some browsers also hand the page a low default resolution. Check the reported resolution and aspect ratio here before blaming the camera.
What if my webcam isn't working?
Close other apps that may be using the camera, check that an external webcam is plugged in and switched on, update your drivers, and make sure both your browser and your operating system allow camera access.
Where should I place my webcam?
Just above eye level, tilted slightly down, so your face and upper body fill the frame with a little headroom. Fullscreen makes a poor angle easy to spot.
Is the fullscreen webcam test private?
Yes. The video is shown locally in your browser. Nothing is uploaded or recorded, and there's no sign-up.
